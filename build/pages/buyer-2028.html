EU disclosure from 2028

The 2028 Buyer

From 1 January 2028, every new building over 1,000 m² in the EU must disclose its life cycle carbon. This page sets out what public sources show about the design teams this rule reaches. It also says what I would check with One Click LCA's own data.

Each section links to the appendix page with the sources and the full model.

1Countries

Countries with a rule today and in 2028

Seven EU countries require a life cycle carbon calculation today. The other twenty, including Germany, Italy and Spain, have no national rule. The EU rule reaches all twenty on 1 January 2028.

What I take from it

Much of the new demand in 2028 is likely to come from design teams that have not bought an LCA tool before. Many will need to learn how an LCA works before they compare tools. I would check this against One Click LCA's pipeline by country.

2Lost deals

Where lost deals go

A design team facing its first disclosure can hire a consultant for each project, use a free national tool or wait until a client asks. Each of these delays buying a licence. I expect many of these deals to end with no decision.

What I take from it

If that is right, these buyers need a reason to start before the deadline. Battlecards against named competitors would help less. If the CRM does not record no decision as a loss reason, I would add it so this can be measured.

On Capterra, value for money scores 4.0 of 5 and support scores 4.9. Value for money is the lowest of the sub-scores. A buyer new to LCA may find it hard to judge what a licence is worth.

What this means for 2027

  1. Explain the method first

    Most of these buyers have not run an LCA. The first message would explain the EU method and how to complete a first project. Webinars with green building councils may reach them more easily than product pages.
