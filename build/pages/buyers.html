Users and buyers

Buyers

The listing asks for conversations with users and with buyers. They are different people. The user runs the LCA and judges the tool by the hours it takes. The buyer signs for it and judges it by the permit, the bid, the certification or the sale it secures. This page maps both for each segment.

Built from public reviews, case studies and forum threads, and from my experience selling sustainability services to these firms at Foster + Partners, SOM and WSP. Criteria weights are my judgement, to be tested in interviews.

In their words

What customers praise and what they complain about

Praise

Database and traceability

"The database is very robust — the materials are tied to an EPD you can open and clearly see global warming potential and other impacts... everything is very traceable." Building sciences engineer, Page, case study

Easy for non-experts

"Designed in a way that even someone with no LCA background can figure it out." Operations manager, OTB Materials, case study

Support

Capterra scores customer service 4.9 out of 5, its highest sub-score. G2 reviewers name individual support staff.

Growth, not only compliance

"That moment turned into a serious business discussion. The tool enables not just compliance, but growth." OTB Materials, case study

Complaints

Revit workflow

"We've found Tally's Revit plug-in much easier to use than OneClick's version." Carbon Leadership Forum, 2023

Learning curve

"The platform has a steep learning curve and some workflows (imports, baselines, compliance paths) are not very intuitive without training." G2, mid-market user, translated

Price and licences

Value for money is the lowest Capterra sub-score at 4.0. Reviewers mention annual upfront payment and confusing user and licence management.

EPD verification

"The EPD Hub verification process and buying credits is cumbersome and could be simplified." G2, consultant, translated

The pattern. Customers praise the data and the people, and complain about the interface and the BIM round-trip. Positioning should lead with data and support. Launches that improve the workflow, such as AI mapping and the new interface, are the ones to put in front of evaluators who have tried Tally.

Interview programme

How I would run buyer and user conversations

  1. Sample by segment, outcome and alternativeEach quarter, ten wins, ten losses and five churned or downgraded accounts, spread across the priority segments. Losses to free tools and to national tools are sampled on purpose.
  2. Interview the signer and the user separatelyThe signer explains why money was spent. The user explains where the time went. Thirty minutes each, the same eight questions every time so answers can be compared.
  3. Tag every call the same wayTrigger, alternative considered, criteria in order, objection, proof that worked. Tags go into the CRM next to the deal so win rate can be cut by them.
  4. Return the findings within two weeksA one-page summary to product, sales and marketing, with the three changes it suggests to messaging, enablement or the roadmap.
