Effective use of AI across the workflow

AI Workflow

The listing asks for effective use of AI. This site is the example. Four research agents ran in parallel on the company, competitors, regulation and customer voice, each writing a sourced file with every claim flagged as verified or unverified. I reviewed their findings, wrote the pages and built the interactive models in one working session with Claude Code.

How this site was made

From brief to published pages

  1. Brief the agents like colleaguesEach agent got one topic, the sources to prefer, the output format and a rule: mark anything unverified and invent nothing.
  2. Run them in parallelCompany and products, competitors, regulation by country, and team and customer voice. Each wrote a markdown file with source links and dates, kept in the repository.
  3. Check what came backThe agents corrected the brief where it was wrong: SimaPro is now part of the group, Tally changed owner in April 2026 and some names in the brief do not exist. Conflicts between sources are flagged on the pages.
  4. Write and buildPages are generated from short source files by a small Python build, with shared navigation, dark mode and an accessibility check.

Control over trust. The CEO's line on AI is "it's not based on trust. It's based on verification and control mechanism guardrails." The same rule applies to marketing research: every claim keeps its source, and unverified claims stay labelled until a person checks them.

In the role

Routines I would set up

Competitor and regulation watch

A weekly agent run over competitor sites, funding news, programme operator rules and regulation trackers. It produces a dated digest with links and flags changes that affect a battlecard or the launch calendar.

Call and review mining

Sales call transcripts and new reviews are tagged for trigger, alternative, objection and proof that worked, feeding the win and loss review.

Launch kit drafting

From the launch brief, first drafts of the battlecard, FAQ, release article and partner email in each launch language, then reviewed by a subject expert before use.

Proof point checks

A monthly scan of the website, decks and partner kits for claims that disagree with the proof point sheet, such as the number of standards, datasets or integrations.
