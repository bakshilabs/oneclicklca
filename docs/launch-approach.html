Taking a release to market

Launch Approach

These are the steps I follow to plan and run a launch. The example throughout is the EU requirement for new buildings to disclose their life cycle carbon.

The example would build on the EU work One Click LCA already does, including its regulation webinars and compliance releases. I would confirm the scope and timing against the product roadmap with product and sales.

My approach

Six steps

What I learn in the last step feeds the choice of who the next launch is for.

Each step

How I do it, with an example

1

Choose who it is for

How I do it

I compare possible segments on the same questions:

  • How close is the deadline that makes them act?
  • Can our sellers and partners reach these buyers?
  • How well does the product fit their need today?
  • How strong are the alternatives they already have?
  • Is there a seller or partner who can carry the message?

I agree the choice with product and sales before any material is written.

Example: EU disclosure

The first group would be design teams in EU countries that have no national rule yet. Many of them will need to learn how an LCA works before they compare tools.

Global firms that already use One Click LCA in one office are a second route. For them the launch is about extending to more offices.

German teams are harder. Free national tools exist and some projects already need an LCA for funding, so the case has to be about time and working across countries.

Manufacturers would get their own launch around the construction product rules.

2

Find the message in buyers' own words

How I do it

I interview buyers and prospects we lost, and I listen to sales calls. I write down the words buyers use for the problem and for what they want at the end of it.

I then draft a short message and test it in real calls. It goes on the website only after sellers have seen it work.

Example: EU disclosure

I would talk to design teams facing their first disclosure, and to teams who chose to wait or to hire a consultant.

Draft message to testOne model, reported to the EU method and to your national scheme.

I would test it in sales calls in the first countries before it is used anywhere else.

3

Decide the size of the launch

How I do it

Company-wide launch. The whole company takes part, with an event, press and full seller training.

Segment launch. Aimed at named segments, with targeted campaigns and seller briefings.

Release note. Help pages, an in-product notice and a line for sellers.

I decide on reach, on the revenue at stake and on whether the message to buyers changes.

Example: EU disclosure

It reaches many new buyers in several countries and changes what sellers say. That makes it a candidate for a company-wide launch.

I would confirm the size with product and sales once the roadmap is clear.

4

Equip sellers and partners

How I do it

I give sellers material they can use on a call without me. I train them on the buyer's problem and the business case before launch day.

Partners get the same material, adapted for their markets.

Example: EU disclosure

A business case that compares a consultant, or waiting, with a licence
A discovery guide to find the trigger and the person who signs
An explainer of the EU method for buyers new to LCA
A battlecard for calls where a free national tool is the alternative
A demo of a first project, from model to report
Briefings for green building councils and resellers
5

Launch and listen

How I do it

On launch day the work moves to listening. I follow the first deals, the questions sellers get and the questions that reach support.

Where buyers do not respond to a message, I change it and tell sellers what changed and why.

Example: EU disclosure

A live event and a webinar with green building councils, material in each local language, and a customer story from an early project.

I would read the first deals in each country to see which alternative buyers chose and what made them wait.

6

Measure and adjust

How I do it

I agree the measures with sales before launch, so we judge the launch on the same terms.

To separate the launch from normal variation, I compare with a period before it. I state what we can claim and what we cannot.

Example: EU disclosure

  • Win rate in the chosen segments.
  • How many deals end with no decision.
  • How many new accounts finish a first project.

If the CRM does not yet record no decision as a loss reason, I would ask to add it before launch.

Risks

Risks and how I would respond

National rules arrive late or differ

Some countries may publish their rules late, and each may set them differently.

My responseThe launch rests on the EU method. Country guides follow as each national rule is published, as smaller releases.

Buyers wait until the deadline is close

A deadline that feels far away does not move a design team to buy.

My responseA second campaign closer to the deadline, timed for next year's budgets and led by a customer story from the first projects.

Free national tools

In some countries a free tool is enough to start.

My responseThe business case compares time per project and one model across countries. Firms that work in more than one country gain the most.
