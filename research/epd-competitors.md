# EPD Generator alternatives: how they message to construction product manufacturers

Researched 11 November 2026 from public homepages and product pages. Quotes are as returned by page fetches; wording may have shifted since. Items marked UNVERIFIED come from third-party listings, search snippets or pages that could not be fetched directly.

## Baseline: One Click LCA EPD Generator (for comparison)

URL: https://oneclicklca.com/en-gb/software/manufacturing/epd-generator

- Headline: "Create EPDs faster, across your entire portfolio"
- Sub-headline: "Get EPDs verified and published in days, not months"
- Claims: "Deliver EPDs in weeks, not months"; "Meet buyer EPD requests without losing the deal"; "Catch data inconsistencies before verification, avoiding costly rework"; "Fills gaps with verified defaults"; "No LCA expertise required."
- Buyer: manufacturers, with explicit support for sales and tender teams.
- Specification/sales/design: yes. Page mentions insight into which projects specify an EPD and helping specifiers add EPDs into live project models. This is the only generator page found that links the EPD to downstream project models.
- Note: the "products without an EPD get penalising generic values" argument is not on this product page. It does appear in One Click LCA editorial content aimed at specifiers (see section on the open angle below).

### Building Transparency EC3 (designer-side database, North America)

URLs: https://www.buildingtransparency.org/ , https://www.buildingtransparency.org/resources/how-get-epd (search snippet; page 404 on fetch), https://www.buildingtransparency.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/carbon_priced_procurement_white_paper.pdf

- Headline: "The next generation of EC3 is here" (EC3 2.0 Beta): "faster, more intuitive experience, improved data quality and expanded global coverage."
- Claims: "the free, open-access EC3 tool and its underlying digitized EPD database"; "encourage low-carbon specification and procurement"; users can "search, filter, and compare building products".
- Manufacturer-facing (from search snippets of the How to Get an EPD guidance, UNVERIFIED as exact wording): when a supplier lacks a product EPD, EC3 assigns "a conservative estimate of impact", on the high side of the industry, placing "the burden of doubt on the declarer", so "most manufacturers will benefit from a product-specific EPD". EC3 uses a value "at least 80% likely to be conservative". Manufacturers can view search and specification statistics for their products. Buy Clean filters let manufacturers see which products appear to comply.
- Carbon-priced procurement white paper describes default assumptions as deliberately adverse, made moot when a bidder submits an acceptable EPD (paraphrase from search result, UNVERIFIED wording).
- Significance: EC3 is a non-commercial body that states the penalising-default argument plainly, but it is not selling EPD creation, so the argument sits on the buyer side and is not turned into a manufacturer call to action by any tool vendor.

### Regulators and databases that state the default-value penalty (context, not competitors)

- Netherlands NMD, https://milieudatabase.nl/en/database/why-a-category-1-or-2-environmental-declaration-in-the-nmd/ : category 3 generic data carries a "30% uplift factor"; manufacturer category 1 or 2 data means "your product becomes part of the basis for design choices and procurement decisions" and is "more visible to designers and developers".
- Sweden Boverket, https://www.boverket.se/sv/klimatdeklaration/gor-sa-har/underlag/klimatdata-till-berakningen/ : generic climate database values are set conservatively, around 25% above typical values (via search summary; exact wording UNVERIFIED). Note a September 2026 Boverket report proposes repealing current climate declaration legislation (UNVERIFIED whether adopted).
- Finland's national emissions database is reported to apply a similar uplift (UNVERIFIED).
- Vermont draft code R408 (insulation) uses industry-average defaults that product data can replace only when lower (https://publicservice.vermont.gov/sites/dps/files/documents/SECTION%20R408%20Insulation%20Embodied%20Carbon%20Calculation_DRAFT.pdf).

## Open angles nobody claims clearly

1. The default-value penalty as a sales argument. No EPD generator, startup or consultant found tells manufacturers: "without an EPD, your product is already in the designer's model at a conservative, inflated default (30% in NMD, about 25% in Boverket, high-side 80th percentile in EC3), so you lose on carbon before you lose on price." Ecochain's generic-data argument is about rival EPDs, not about having no EPD. The argument exists only in regulator, database and One Click LCA editorial content, never on a generator product page. One Click LCA's own generator page even uses "verified defaults" as a positive feature, which sits awkwardly next to this angle and would need careful wording.
2. Design-stage visibility inside the designer's tool. Only One Click LCA can credibly connect the EPD creation side to the building LCA where materials are chosen (its generator page hints at it). Competitors stop at "publish to programme operators". C.Scale and EC3 own visibility on the designer side but do not create EPDs.
3. Demand evidence (which projects and specifiers are looking at your category). EC3 offers search and specification statistics; no generator vendor sells this to manufacturers in headline copy.
4. Commercial buyer as the primary audience. Every vendor still leads with sustainability teams; Emidat and Ecochain add sales as a secondary section. Nobody leads with the commercial director's problem (losing specifications or tenders to products with EPDs).
5. Transparent data-quality metrics. CarbonCloud's "Primary Data Share" and "Data Quality Rating" have no equivalent in construction EPD messaging.
6. Cost of not having an EPD (as opposed to cost of making one). All cost claims are about cheaper EPDs; none quantifies lost specifications.
