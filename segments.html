Choosing who a launch is for

Segment Model

The listing asks the product marketer to choose priority sub-segments for each launch. With 170 countries, a dozen roles and dozens of rules, every release suits someone. This model scores fifteen sub-segments on six factors, and the weights change with the type of launch.

Scores are my judgement from the research on the Demand Drivers, Buyers and Alternatives pages. In the role I would replace them with CRM data on pipeline, win rate and deal size.

    Factors

    What each factor measures

    Regulatory pressure

    How close and how binding the next forcing date is. A limit value in force scores 5; a voluntary scheme scores 1 or 2.

    Deal value

    Expected annual contract value per account, from a single Business licence to firm-wide Power Pack and enterprise deals.

    Reachable volume

    How many accounts the sales team and partners can reach in the next two quarters.

    Product fit

    How well the current product meets the segment's need today, including local compliance, language and integrations.

    Room to win

    The inverse of competitive pressure. A free national tool or a strong local incumbent lowers this score.

    Channel readiness

    Whether a local team, reseller, association or programme operator is ready to carry the message.

    How I would use it. Before each tier one or tier two launch, product, sales and product marketing agree the weights first and the scores second. Agreeing weights first keeps the debate on what the launch is for. The top two or three segments get the campaign, the enablement and the adoption target. The rest get the release note.
