Why buyers buy

Demand Drivers

Most One Click LCA purchases start with a date in a regulation, a certification scheme or a tender. This page lists those dates from 2025 to 2031, shows which buyer each one forces to act, and turns the calendar into launch windows.

Status checked against official and secondary sources on 23 October 2026. Items marked "to confirm" rest on one secondary source.

The shape of demand

Three waves of forced buyers

Now

National leaders

Denmark, Finland, the Netherlands, France, Sweden, Iceland and Ireland's public sector already require whole life carbon calculations, and most set limit values. Buyers here know what LCA is. They compare tools, often against a cheap national tool.

What wins

Local compliance, speed per project and switching cost.

2028

The EU-wide step

From 1 January 2028 every new building over 1,000 m² in the EU and EEA must disclose life cycle global warming potential in its energy certificate. For the first time this reaches design teams in Germany, Italy, Spain, Poland and the rest of the EU, most of whom have never bought an LCA tool.

What wins

Education, an easy first project and a trusted EU method.

2030

All buildings and products

From 2030 disclosure covers all new buildings and national limit values apply. Construction product declarations under the revised CPR arrive family by family, cement first, with digital product passports to follow.

What wins

Scale, automation and product data that flows from manufacturer to project.

What this means for product marketing. The second wave is the largest group of new buyers the company has faced. National roadmaps with limit values are due on 1 January 2027, which gives about fourteen months between the roadmaps and the first disclosure deadline. A campaign and launch plan for first-time buyers in the large EU markets should be ready by the start of 2027.

Where demand is softer

Markets where the push is voluntary

United States

Federal Buy Clean funding for low-carbon materials was rescinded in July 2025. Demand rests on California, a few states and cities, and LEED v5. EC3 is free and the default reference for many designers.

United Kingdom

Part Z is still not adopted and the Future Homes Standard covers operational energy only. The London Plan and RICS whole life carbon standard drive demand, and a second tool, CarboniCa, is now RICS validated.

Middle East and Asia Pacific

No binding UAE rule was found. Demand comes from certification schemes such as LEED, Estidama, GSAS and Mostadam, Green Star, Green Mark and the voluntary NABERS embodied carbon rating.

Message implication. Where rules force the purchase, the message is compliance done faster and with less risk. Where demand is voluntary, the message has to be the bid won, the certification credit gained or the client target met, because nobody is fined for doing nothing.
